# Watchtower briefing: Monday 23 November 2026

Covers what is new since the 16 November report. Not legal or tax advice; each item says who to check with.

## Headline
Three things to get right before the own shop launches: the "no returns on personalised items" wording, how we word "cheaper than Etsy", and how the vote page collects email addresses. Separately, Etsy's new photo rules (from 22 October) seem fine for our print-on-demand listings, but they matter for the planned recoloured lifestyle photos.

## Act on this

**1. Personalised "In Person" garments and the 14-day returns right (before launch)**
- **The rule:** Online buyers in the UK normally get 14 days to change their mind. The law makes an exception for goods that are "clearly personalised", and a name printed on a shirt is the government's own example. **But the exception only counts if the shop tells the buyer before they pay** that they can't cancel.
- **What it means for us:** "In Person" garments can be no-returns for change of mind. This partly solves the open decision on returns that Printify won't take back. Plain designs are not covered, and that includes a plain design in the same basket as a personalised one. Faulty or misprinted items must still be replaced or refunded under the Consumer Rights Act, personalised or not.
- **Proposed change:** Add one clear line on each "In Person" design page and on the basket page before checkout: "Personalised with your name, so it can't be returned if you change your mind. Faulty or misprinted? We'll put it right." Put the same wording in the returns page. This is a shop worker change, so it needs your OK.
- **Who to check with:** Business Companion (free Trading Standards guidance) or Citizens Advice's business pages.

**2. "About 20% cheaper than on Etsy" (before launch)**
- **The rule:** Under the consumer law that came in in April 2025 (the DMCC Act), price comparisons must be true and stay true. In July the High Court rejected the competition watchdog's strict "fixed ratio" test for "was/now" prices. That makes these claims a matter of judgement rather than a fixed formula, which isn't the same as being more relaxed.
- **What it means for us:** Saying "cheaper than our Etsy shop" is fine if it is true for each item at the moment a shopper sees it. The risk is that Etsy prices change, or an Etsy sale runs, and the claim goes stale. Etsy prices already include free postage, and so do ours, so we are comparing like with like.
- **Proposed change:** Use a general line ("Our own shop prices are lower than on Etsy") rather than a fixed percentage or crossed-out Etsy prices. Have the Finance agent check the line monthly against the live Etsy prices. Don't put any own-shop mention on Etsy itself (Etsy rule, see last week's report).
- **Who to check with:** Business Companion's pricing guidance (the CTSI pricing practices guide).

**3. "Vote on the next gag" page and the 5% code (before launch)**
- **The rule:** UK marketing-email rules (PECR) now carry fines up to £17.5 million, the same as data protection law. The ICO can fine for any breach, not just serious ones (since 5 February 2026). The "soft opt-in" that lets you email past customers doesn't cover people who have only voted.
- **What it means for us:** If the page collects email addresses to send the code, sending them anything later (new gags, offers) needs a clear, unticked opt-in box. If the page adds a tracking or advertising cookie, it needs a cookie banner. The privacy page must also say what the email is used for.
- **Proposed change:** Show the 5% code on screen after voting, so no email is needed. If you want a mailing list, add a separate, unticked "Tell me about new gags" box. Keep the page cookie-free apart from what the basket needs. Needs your OK (shop worker).
- **Who to check with:** the ICO's small business helpline (free).

**4. Etsy's new photo rules (announced 22 October, enforcement reported for early 2027)**
- **What changed:** Most Etsy listings now need at least one real, unedited photo of the item. Print-on-demand listings are an exception and can use a mockup instead, as long as you own or are licensed to use it and it shows what the buyer actually gets. AI-made patterns and fully bot-written listings are banned. Sources disagree on whether AI-edited mockups or backgrounds need to be disclosed. Etsy reportedly tweaked the photo wording on 6 November.
- **What it means for us:** Our own mockups should be fine. The risk is the plan to make lifestyle scenes by recolouring garment photos with AI: a scene must show the real garment colour, and the first photo should be the clearest, most honest view of the item.
- **Proposed change:** The Etsy agent checks (read-only) that each live listing's first photo is a plain, accurate mockup. Keep AI-recoloured scenes out of the first slot and label them in the description ("colour shown digitally"). Re-check Etsy's own Listing Image Requirements page before you make the recoloured scenes.
- **Who to check with:** Etsy's Listing Image Requirements help page (Shop Manager > Help).

## Keep an eye on
- **Christmas posting for the own shop:** Royal Mail's last recommended UK dates are 2nd Class 18 Dec, Tracked 48 19 Dec, 1st Class 21 Dec, Special Delivery 23 Dec. Print Clever said last December that its production time goes from 3 to 5 working days in peak season. If the shop is live before Christmas, a "order by about 14 December for Christmas" note would be safe. Printify's own 2026 deadlines still haven't been found.
- **Etsy money timing:** Etsy is moving to holding its sale fees in your pending balance until the sale clears, and has removed the separate "reserve minimum". Your "current balance" can now show as negative. This doesn't change what you pay, but the Finance agent should allow for it in the December check.
- **Etsy UK Regulatory Operating Fee:** this went up from 0.32% to 0.48% of the order total on 22 June 2026. It isn't new, but it wasn't in last week's report, so the Finance agent should use 0.48%.
- **Tax:** no new HMRC changes this week. Paying tax in regular instalments instead of January and July lump sums is planned from April 2029 (consultation stage). Making Tax Digital dates are as in last week's report.
- **Still waiting:** the government's response on the UK product safety overhaul hasn't been published yet.
- **No change:** Stripe UK fees (1.5% + 20p), US de minimis, EU parcel charges, UK VAT threshold (£90,000). No sign of Gildan 5000 / 2400 / 18000 / 18500 being dropped. Printify only removed a mentoring perk from its paid plans. No trade mark for "Introvert Here" found.

## Market notes
- Personalised T-shirts are named a strong 2026 category (Kittl trend piece), which supports the "In Person" range.
- "Gift for introverts" searches peak in December (about 170 a month last year). "Introvert tshirt" searches were down about 18% year on year to September (third-party estimates).
- Rival themes this week include "Too Peopley" and "Homebody". Many introvert shops are small, so there's still room in the niche.
- Printful (Printify's sister company) now sells through Shopify's AI shopping assistant. AI shopping assistants are a channel to watch, but there's nothing to do yet.
